Why Netflix

I’m Muhammed, a Harvard engineering student applying to be a Software Engineer Intern, Summer 2027. It starts in a house with no TV.

Scroll

01 / 06

A house with no TV

Growing up, we never had a TV, and I wasn’t allowed to go to the movie theater. I was told there were too many bad things in movies. I loved film and television anyway.

02 / 06

A dusty old computer

I watched whatever I could find on YouTube on our dusty old computer, lived for the days teachers put on a movie, made my own stop-motion films, and ran around dressed as a superhero making up stories.

03 / 06

My first movie theater

When I was old enough to decide for myself, I saw my first movie in a theater: Spider-Man: No Way Home. I still remember how transformed I felt walking out.

04 / 06

My own account

When I started earning my own money, I bought my own Netflix subscription. That endless list of movies and shows was overwhelming in the best way.

05 / 06

Then, the stage

Film stayed my favorite way to learn. I even wrote my college essay about it. In college, facing what I wanted my future to look like, I realized my whole life had been pointing to this field. I started acting and was cast as a lead in a short film and a play. Now when I watch something, I think about everything that went into making it.

06 / 06

The part nobody sees

That’s part of why I built VBR Lab. Streaming is the piece of the magic most viewers never see, the engineering that makes a show start instantly and never stall, and it’s the piece I want to help build.

Netflix gave me an outlet early in my life. Being part of making that magic for others would be an incredible honor.
